# Correspondence to the original AIM 648 target

Original statement: [Location of the most isolated point in a random polyhedral sample](https://github.com/MColbrook/AIM/blob/a153f469ee0ea64e876b2ad97c79d252c19a3e22/problems/648-polyhedral-most-isolated-point-location.md), revision `a153f469ee0ea64e876b2ad97c79d252c19a3e22`.

The manuscript claims the affirmative alternative in both strict angle
regimes. The comparison below uses its published mathematical argument and
the original public statement.

| Original requirement | Manuscript location | Coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every compact convex polyhedron in R^3 with nonempty interior | Section 1 and Theorem 1; Lemma 3 | Every fixed A; nonsimple vertices included |
| Independent uniform sample and actual n-point nearest-neighbour distances | Section 1 definitions; Proposition 5, formula (15) | Original fixed-size sample; n>=2 |
| Smallest index attaining the largest nearest-neighbour distance | Section 1 definition of I_n; Section 5 comparison of strict and nonstrict restricted maxima | Original tie rule, including possible mutual-nearest-neighbour ties |
| Minimum interior dihedral angle greater than pi/2 | Proposition 4, face regime; Theorem 1 | Normalized H^2 surface-area measure on all of the boundary |
| Minimum interior dihedral angle below pi/2 | Proposition 4, edge regime, formulas (4), (6), (9), (11); Theorem 1 | Normalized H^1 length measure on every minimum-angle edge, with arbitrary unequal edge lengths |
| Convergence in distribution of the actual selected point | Section 5, including zero/one-measure continuity sets | Weak convergence in R^3 |
| Critical equality excluded | Theorem 1 and Section 1 | No equality-case claim added |

The manuscript proves its polyhedral spatial-intensity and joint binomial
no-exceedance statements. The original leading strong law is contextual
attribution, and the later smooth-domain/planar-polygon fluctuation theorem is
methodological context. Neither is used as a three-dimensional polyhedral
location theorem. Basic integration and weak-convergence tools have ordinary
public references in the manuscript.

This is a complete analytic solution claim for independent review. It is not
an assertion of official AIM acceptance, formal verification or human review.
