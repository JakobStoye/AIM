## Independent reconstruction of the construction

The real Hilbert space indexed by reduced words in involutive color letters supplies commuting left and right involutions for each color. Their common positive eigenspace projection is $`P_i=(I+L_i)(I+R_i)/4`$. Conjugation by word parity gives the common negative projection $`Q_i`$, and $`P_iQ_i=0`$. The vacuum vectors $`u_i=e+e_i`$ and $`v_i=e-e_i`$ have the required opposite-color inner products: zero for the same color and one for different colors.

I checked the main inverse lemma, including its infinite-dimensional use. Expand the sum of tensor projections into the $`4^d`$ left/right patterns. For any nontrivial pattern orient a nonloop matrix entry from the smaller tuple of words to the larger in total word length, breaking ties consistently. At any tuple, at most $`2d`$ labels can produce a nonlarger neighbor: such a label must be the first or last letter of an active nonempty coordinate. All other labels increase length. An all-empty coordinate fixed by a left-right pair contributes to the diagonal, not the oriented matrix. The oriented matrix has row sum at most $`q`$ and column sum at most $`2d`$, including multiplicities. Schur's bound therefore gives off-diagonal norm at most $`2\sqrt{2dq}`$ after averaging. The diagonal is between $`q/4^d`$ and $`q`$. For the stated color bound the Neumann inverse exists with norm at most $`2\cdot4^d/q`$. These are bounded operators on the full word space; no finite truncation is assumed.

The proposed approximate inverse applied to the vacuum has a residual consisting of $`q(2^d-1)`$ distinct orthogonal coordinate vectors. Its norm and the inverse bound give exactly the manuscript's $`C_dq^{-3/2}`$ approximation error. Contracting slots against the vacuum preserves each remaining projection range. Consequently the tensors sum over colors to the vacuum tensor in every valence, including zero, and the parity-conjugate tensors have the same identity. This proves vertex-deletion consistency even at branching vertices. Orthogonality of the two projection ranges makes every monochromatic edge have weight zero.
