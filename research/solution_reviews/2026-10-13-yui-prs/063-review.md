## Complete independent reconstruction

**Boundary action and area.** For an actual simple metric, first variation gives $`D_s=-p`$, $`D_t=q`$ with unreversed exit covector. The normal Jacobi equation has positive solution to exit; pairing endpoint variation with the rotated exit direction yields $`t_p=-j(\tau)/(r\sin\theta)`$, hence $`D_{st}>0`$. This makes the endpoint map a smooth diffeomorphism. The smooth full scattering involution includes tangencies; the endpoint-distance profile extends smoothly from either diagonal side with slopes $`1`$ and $`-1`$. Its primitive is the boundary distance. The incoming measure is $`ds\,|dp|`$, so Santaló gives the exact factor $`\mathop{\mathrm{Area}}\nolimits(M)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int D D_{st}`$. The explicitly worked Euclidean disk example has the correct signs, flight time and area $`\pi R^2`$.

**Boundary holomorphic coordinate, lines 427–568.** Smooth scattering-invariant boundary functions extend to smooth first integrals on a simple surface. Zeroth-mode surjectivity gives a first integral with any chosen smooth holomorphic base mode. Symmetrizing directions removes odd modes; its nonnegative-mode projection stays invariant because its only possible negative mode is the vanishing antiholomorphic derivative. Conversely, an invariant even boundary function with no negative modes has a smooth invariant extension. Its negative-mode part has zero full boundary trace and its transport derivative has only mode $`-1`$. The cited single-mode injectivity forces that part to vanish. The remaining $`-1`$ equation proves the zeroth mode holomorphic. Thus the boundary Fourier test gives exactly holomorphic traces, with no assumption of an unknown conformal interior coordinate.

Smooth uniformization gives an initial disk diffeomorphism. A smooth holomorphic function whose boundary trace is a degree-one orientation-preserving circle diffeomorphism maps the disk bijectively onto itself: the argument principle gives one preimage of each interior value and none of any exterior value; an interior unit-circle value is excluded by the same local count. Multiplicity one and its nonzero boundary tangential derivative give a smooth inverse up to the boundary. Three cyclic marks uniquely normalize a disk automorphism by the displayed Cayley maps. The boundary arclength relation then determines exactly $`b=1/|\phi_s|`$.

**Canonical density selection.** The boundary action is bounded by shorter boundary arclength because its end profiles vanish and $`|D_t|<1`$. In the selected angular coordinate it is bounded by $`(\pi/2)\max b`$ times the endpoint chord length. A constant positive density therefore dominates every requested joining curve, proving universal nonempty feasibility. Linear curve inequalities and positivity make this set convex; its strong $`L^2`$ closure has a unique least-norm class by the parallelogram/Cauchy minimizing-sequence argument. Continuous representatives are unique, including boundary values. No curve is evaluated on a general $`L^2`$ representative.

The fixed geodesic equations were recomputed from the conformal Christoffel symbols: their angular sign, curvature $`-\rho^{-2}\Delta\log\rho`$, and boundary-convexity expression are correct. Testing finite exit for every interior and inward boundary state gives forward/backward nontrapping. Testing the scalar Jacobi solution on every subsegment excludes every conjugate pair, including endpoints. Strict convexity, nontrapping and absence of conjugate points on the disk are equivalent to simplicity by the cited theorem. Thus C4 tests precisely simplicity of the already-selected smooth metric.

**Necessity of the canonical minimizer, lines 655–687.** The actual conformal density belongs to the feasible set. Integrating every other feasible density's length inequality over its geodesics and applying Santaló yields $`\langle\sigma,\rho\rangle\ge\|\rho\|^2`$. This linear pairing passes to the strong closure. Expanding the square proves $`\|u\|^2\ge\|\rho\|^2+\|u-\rho\|^2`$, making the actual density uniquely least norm. Its smooth positivity, prescribed boundary value, simplicity and action-area equality give every condition.

**Strong closure and sufficient area saturation, lines 691–803.** For feasible smooth approximants converging in $`L^2`$ to the selected smooth density, Santaló and Cauchy–Schwarz make their length errors along its entire geodesic family tend to zero in incoming $`L^1`$. Thus the selected metric's full boundary distance dominates the proposed action, despite the absence of a continuous individual-curve trace on $`L^2`$.

On the periodic smooth zero-ended profile rectangle, $`\partial_s\partial_\delta-\partial_\delta^2`$ is self-adjoint by the written boundary integrations. Hence the difference of the two quadratic action areas equals the integral of the nonnegative distance difference times the sum of their strictly positive mixed derivatives.
