## Complete independent argument audit

1. The period-two unfolding is continuous at both walls and piecewise $`C^1`$. Direct substitution gives $`z_t+z_x=a\theta-\partial_xQ(z,z\circ(-\mathrm{id}))`$, with $`Q(A,B)=(3A^2-2AB-B^2)/8`$. The flux is zero at each wall, so there is no missing distributional wall source. Straight characteristics cross walls only finitely many times and give the exact integrated formula (source lines 120-182).
2. The linear response is one-antiperiodic and its telescoping primitive formula bounds it by $`7\|U\|_\infty`$. The $`C^1`$ smallness bound controls the quadratic flux derivative. Absorbing the resulting estimate gives $`\|z\|_\infty\le14A`$ and $`\|z-\ell\|_\infty\le45\varepsilon A`$, without bounding the derivative of the control. Polarization of Q gives the stated $`3(M+M')\delta/4`$ difference bound (184-207).
3. For an exact linear return generated by $`U=F+F(\cdot-1)`$, substitution in Q gives the full expansion $`3P^2/2-2PU+PR+U^2/2-R^2/2`$. Partitioning the time integral into unit intervals yields exactly $`\Gamma_\ell=(3/2)G_F(T-x)+C`$. The integrals of U and R supply the full $`FJ_F`$ term; the shifted length-two intervals account for the entire R-squared term. No quadratic contribution is omitted (209-267).
4. At zero tank displacement the corrected primitive has zero mean. On the long interval $`[d,1]`$ this makes $`G_F=F^2`$. On the short overlap, both cross terms are retained. The separation lemma bounds the oscillation below by a fixed multiple of $`M^2`$, uses the zero plateau to control overlap energy and the mean, and then uses the long positive plateau to force a contradictory signed mean. All estimates allow arbitrary control frequencies and either sign of F; the constants at $`d=1/10`$ check (273-332).
5. The actual nonlinear terminal target has no antiperiodic component. Its antiperiodic projection bounds the primitive's terminal tail. Linear endpoint interpolation followed by a compactly supported unit-integral correction produces a mean-zero exact-return primitive with error at most $`100\varepsilon A`$. The corresponding control primitive differs by at most $`200\varepsilon A`$, and $`A\le4M`$ (334-379).
6. The auxiliary linear response differs from the actual state by at most $`1450\varepsilon A`$. Polarization bounds the endpoint primitive error by $`1{,}100{,}000\varepsilon M^2`$. The exact periodic terminal identity then bounds the separation norm by $`(2/3)1{,}100{,}000\varepsilon M^2`$. At $`\varepsilon=10^{-8}`$ its coefficient is $`11/1500<1/100`$, contradicting the separation lemma. If $`M=0`$, the state estimates instead force the nonzero target to vanish (381-427).
7. The periodic smooth target has zero mean; its even/odd parts obey the mass and wall constraints and equal endpoint height derivatives. Its $`C^1`$ norm is $`O(\rho)`$ and height stays positive for small rho. Every endpoint neighborhood thus contains an excluded state at the same fixed time and size bound, which is the required negation of local controllability (401-433).
