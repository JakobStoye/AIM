## Exact target and finite-game interface

The question concerns undirected self-avoiding Trap on the full nearest-neighbor site-percolated lattice, with complete knowledge of the board. The proof establishes absence of draws for every deletion probability strictly between zero and one in dimension two, and a strictly positive draw probability for some strictly positive deletion probability in each dimension at least three. It retains the deleted-origin convention. It does not assert a critical probability or classify every higher-dimensional probability.

The finite bipartite-game criterion is correctly imported: the first player loses precisely when the start belongs to every maximum independent set. The parity-selected exit game is implemented by a private leaf or a private two-edge path at each boundary vertex. I checked the independent-set cardinality of both gadgets, including a closed attachment vertex. Their common additive constants cancel on removing an interior vertex. The resulting zero/one independence-number increment therefore gives exactly the exit outcome. Favorable-exit strategies are monotone with larger domains. A strategy forcing a finite win in the infinite game has a finitely branching history tree with no infinite branch, hence uniformly bounded depth; a finite box contains it. This proves exhaustion independence and the asserted exact characterization of draws by disagreeing limiting boundary outcomes, rather than merely establishing finite-box behavior.

## Planar proof independently reconstructed

The conditional potential is formed from finite differences of independence numbers under changing finitely many open indicators to one. Each telescoping increment eventually stabilizes because the two boundary increments are monotone binary variables. The difference from toggling a site is the conditional expectation of the limiting disagreement indicator at that site. On the even-side-length square, removal of its single annulus disconnects the interior from the distant boundary. Reflection across the half-integer coordinate swaps parity and preserves the conditioned inner board; the unconditioned outside retains its law. The interior differences cancel and the annulus size bounds the potential by its stated surface order. The all-open reference potential has zero conditional parity difference by the same reflection.

Lexicographic exposure gives centered Doob increments equal to the new centered Bernoulli indicator times a conditional expectation of that site's disagreement. The own-coordinate independence of the disagreement indicator justifies this exact formula. For the conditional quadratic variation, approximate the full-past conditional function by finite-coordinate functions in L2. Sites with such a finite neighborhood fully inside the box have the required conditional approximation; the boundary fraction vanishes. The finite-coordinate spatial averages have vanishing variance because distant neighborhoods involve disjoint independent input coordinates. This establishes convergence of the quadratic variation, with variance at least the Bernoulli variance times the squared mean disagreement. It does not require an unproved spatial ergodic claim for the adaptive martingale.

I checked the supplied martingale characteristic-function proof. The compensated exponential has per-step Taylor error of order the volume to the minus three halves, since increments are bounded. Summing makes the error vanish. Bounded quadratic variation and its probability convergence allow replacement of the compensator in L1, giving a centered normal limit with the asserted variance. The normalized potential remains supported in a fixed compact interval by its surface bound. Consequently its normal limit must have zero variance (equivalently, the manuscript's comparison of even moments gives the same contradiction). The positive Bernoulli variance in the stipulated range then forces zero disagreement and zero draw probability. No finite-activity Gibbs uniqueness theorem is being passed to zero temperature without justification.

## Buffered contours, nested topology and transport

The coarse defect cells are neighborhoods of endpoints of empty edges; enlarging them by two cells produces a finite union of separated star components. Every adjacent complementary collar is outside this enlarged set and every adjacent support cell is outside the smaller defect set. A good cell and its fine neighborhood have exactly one occupied endpoint on every edge, so have a unique checkerboard label. Timár's boundary-connectivity theorem and overlap of neighboring good boxes make each complementary collar have one common label, even for a bounded component with more defects farther inside.

I independently checked the nested-hull ascent. If the root is in a wrong good component, its visible exterior boundary lies in one star component and separates it from infinity. If that component has the wrong exterior label, an exterior collar cell is itself in a finite wrong good component. Its visible boundary lies in a distinct support component. Distinct enlarged components are separated by at least two in sup distance, so every star step within either can be interpolated through its unit cube while avoiding the other. Thus the old support lies in the bounded complementary component of the new support containing the collar cell, while the new support lies in the old support's exterior. The new exterior is contained in the old exterior: it is disjoint from the old support and has a path to infinity avoiding it. Filled hulls are therefore strictly nested. A finite set of support components cannot sustain indefinite ascent. This proves selection of a correctly labeled exterior contour enclosing the root, including a root originally in the defect region.

The transport shifts every opposite-labeled bounded component by one fine unit and fills the selected support with its exterior checkerboard. Such components and all interval heads lie inside the free box: otherwise an outward ray in the fixed outside checkerboard would give the opposite collar the exterior label. Every old empty edge outside the selected support has endpoints buffered at coarse distance at least four from it, so shifting by one unit preserves its complementary component. The shifted collars become the exterior checkerboard, creating neither occupied pairs nor new crossing empty edges. All old empty edges inside the support disappear, and none crosses its boundary. Summing the identity for an empty-edge indicator over the finite changed region yields the gain in occupied vertices as the number removed divided by twice the dimension. The support size is at most twice fifteen to the dimension times that edge count, giving exactly the stated positive linear gain.

The per-line cycles extending each shifted interval by its head are disjoint finite permutations supported in the original box. Removing newly occupied deleted sites costs no more than the number of deleted inputs in the enlarged support neighborhood, because their distinct preimages are support sites or neighboring interval tails. This proves the deterministic maximum-cardinality comparison on two permuted boards, including its boundary condition. It does not assume that a random board-selected permutation preserves unbiased fluctuations.

## Critical uniform position entropy: checked, not merely read

The majority approximation error follows by routing ordered pairs through coordinate paths in each cube; congestion is at most side length times cube volume. Opposite-majority adjacent cubes require a positive multiple of side length to the dimension minus one fine cuts in their union. A fine edge is in at most twice the dimension such rectangles. These arguments supply the displayed cutoff $`M=\lfloor F_d k/l^{d-1}\rfloor`$ on the number of coarse cuts.

I separately reconstructed the position encoding for disconnected and nested interfaces. Each root cut rectangle contains a fine cut meeting the selected support; hence its integer fine address is within a dimension constant times l of an attachment cell of that support. A depth-first walk of a star spanning tree has fine-lattice length at most $`4d(k-1)`$. The initial attachment is within $`2d(k-1)`$ of the fixed root because the filled hull is inside the support's bounding rectangle. Ordering interface roots by their first attachment visit bounds the sum of fine-address jump lengths by $`6dk+2(2d+1)lm\le K_dk`$. Here $`m\le M`$ and $`d\ge2`$; the bound has no box-size term.

A code specifies an ordered composition of the total cut count Q, signed root increments of total absolute size at most $`K_dk`$, root orientations, and depth-first walks in the fixed bounded-degree cut-address graph. Each walk visits every cut in its component. Given the fixed anchor, root increments reconstruct absolute positions, and walks reconstruct the complete cut set. A finite cube union is uniquely determined by that cut set and the label zero at infinity: the difference of two possible binary labelings is constant on the connected lattice. Thus codes decode uniquely on valid data, regardless of disconnectedness or nesting. Every attainable union has such a code; nonunique codes for one union merely overcount.

Summing the ordered compositions over $`Q\le M`$ costs at most $`2^M`$, not an omitted factorial. The signed-increment count is bounded by $`2^{dm}\binom{K+dm}{dm}`$; component walks and orientations contribute only exponential-in-M factors. Monotonicity of $`m\log(1+K/m)`$ then gives entropy $`O_d(M+M\log(1+K/M))`$. When M is nonzero, floor comparison gives $`K/M=O_d(l^{d-1})`$; when M is zero the only finite union is empty. The claimed bound $`E_d(1+\ell)k/2^{\ell(d-1)}`$ follows uniformly over the box. This resolves the precise encoding/multiplicity question left in the initial triage.

## Centering, chaining and the original infinite game

For fixed admissible permutations the disagreement-input set has the same image under both maps. Conditional on inputs outside this image, the two remaining permuted arrays have the same independent Bernoulli law, so the independence-number difference is centered. Exposing the differing inputs has bounded differences two. The small-deletion Bernoulli subgaussian proxy tends to zero, and its elementary two-range exponential bound gives the stated concentration estimate. The permutation metric is bounded by the symmetric difference of shifted regions plus their linear-in-support head counts.

Chaining selects actual admissible representatives for each majority-union class before observing the board. At scale zero the representative is the exact region; at the first scale with zero possible cuts every representative is empty. Consecutive representatives have the claimed geometric symmetric-difference bound. The entropy above controls the pair count. The thresholds have a summable geometric first term exactly when dimension exceeds two, and a second term paying the uniform support-size residual exponent. With deletion probability sufficiently small their total is below the linear cardinality gain. Summing over support sizes gives a convergent stretched-exponential series tending to zero as the proxy tends to zero. Independently, a binomial bound for deleted inputs near a support pays the exponential count of rooted supports. All estimates are uniform over the original finite box.

A wrong-root maximum set would select a contour and violate one of these two uniform events. Outside them every even-boundary maximum contains the origin and every odd-boundary maximum omits it. The former also ensures the origin is open. Choosing a fixed positive deletion probability to make the exceptional probability below one half yields a uniform positive boundary-outcome disagreement probability. Those events decrease along exhausting cell boxes, so continuity from above and the already checked finite-game interface give a positive draw probability on the original infinite board. This completes the second clause without changing the rules or dropping the open-origin factor.
