I checked the original Alexandrov class carefully. Planar strict convexity and normalized compact sections supply the baseline local Sobolev Hessian and continuous gradient. The map $`Y=x+Du`$ is injective with Lipschitz inverse. Its image is open by the local minimization argument. The mixed Monge--Ampere identity follows by smooth convex approximation and weak measure convergence, while the already obtained Sobolev regularity identifies its trace term. It yields absolute continuity of the transformed measure and the null-image property for $`Y`$. Applying the Lipschitz area formula to the inverse shows its derivative is nonsingular almost everywhere, with no discarded positive-measure singular set. The inverse differentiation and linewise weak-derivative argument then identify the determinant and Hessian in the original class. In particular, the proof does not assume an unjustified change-of-variables property for a general Sobolev gradient or discard a singular Hessian component.

The reflection and complex-coordinate calculation gives the exact distortion parameter $`K=\lambda^{-1}`$ and cone slope $`k=(K-1)/(K+1)`$. The distance variable is comparable to the reciprocal of one plus the largest Hessian eigenvalue. For every rotation, subtracting that rotation from the reflected inverse map gives an injective open map by strict monotonicity on each fiber. Its analytic distortion is $`K`$. A normalized Riemann map followed by Mori's estimate for the inverse gives the strict Lipschitz deficit on fixed inner disks. The boundary separation is uniform over the compact rotation parameter; no rotation-dependent constant is later treated as uniform without proof.

I reconstructed the weighted scalar projection instead of importing a periodic theorem into a local domain. Its envelope bound has the claimed power of the scale. The conical variation argument proves saturation of the projected gradient on the nonzero residual set. Translation competitors give the weighted variational inequality, including the derivative of the weight. The displayed separating function's matrix inequality follows directly from its two branches. Combining it with the divergence-free rotated second row and integrating by parts produces the stated defect estimate with both weight-error terms retained. On the actual saturated set, the cone bound supplies the weighted energy estimate; Holder's inequality with the cutoff power controls the linear weight derivative. Choosing a finite integer larger than $`K+1`$ gives a defect exponent strictly larger than $`2/(K^2-1)`$ for every $`K>1`$, including parameters near one.

The final scalar integral has the correct negative coefficient when its exponent is below $`(1-k)/k`$. I checked its two branches and the cutoff-dependent uniform bound. Integrating the explicit defect, rather than the noncanonically chosen projections, avoids a measurability assumption about those projections. Fubini is used only with positive lower scale cutoff, followed by monotone convergence. Weighted area change then gives local strong integrability at exactly $`(1+\lambda^2)/(1-\lambda^2)`$, which implies the requested weak all-threshold estimate. A finite cover of the compact transformed interior set gives the full requested localization.
