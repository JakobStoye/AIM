Appendix A supplies rather than assumes compactness over general spectral measures. The semiconvex contact-improvement proof uses a nondegenerate directional cap, a finite difference attaining an upper contact and a distant annular deficit. It controls all intermediate/far tails before choosing the small growth exponent. Cutting the primitive to finite Lipschitz norm costs only the known gradient growth. Interior linear estimates and contact scaling give a uniform local C1 modulus. Weak spectral convergence applies to gradients and differences with integrable tails, never to a primitive whose growth could exceed the operator order.

The new convex cone classification was reconstructed. For a nonzero boundary direction z of the zero cone, convexity makes v=partial_(-z)U nonnegative. If nonzero, strong positivity and a smooth tangent ball give v(z+t nu)>=c t^s. The convex secant inequality instead gives v<=M t. Since s<1 this is impossible. All boundary-direction derivatives vanish, forcing a half-space and a one-dimensional primitive; half-line uniqueness gives the (1+s)-power profile. For general convex zero sets, the blowdown recession cone is treated even if it has empty interior: the strip-removal estimate for locally Lipschitz finite differences makes them entire harmonic, and Liouville forces an affine contradiction. Thus no thin-cone case is discarded.

Radius-normalized approximation is checked without asserting preservation of an arbitrary free-boundary point under convergence. The gradient normalization and its full growth bound force translation zero and the exact profile coefficient in the limit. The variational comparison in the prescribed homogeneous energy class gives boundedness, Lipschitz and semiconvex bounds, with uniqueness from the spectral spanning zero-energy argument. The defect v=u-phi has forcing gradient controlled by the C3 obstacle norm independently of support. Rescaling at the last violating scale makes both this defect and semiconvexity small. The flatness theorem then gives the endpoint contact gradient bound; near/far pair estimates and interior linear estimates give the full global gradient Hölder seminorm.
