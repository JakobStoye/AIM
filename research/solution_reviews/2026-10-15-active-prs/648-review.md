The inball construction gives a uniform positive clipped-ball fraction. I checked the exclusive-ball construction for centers separated between one radius and a fixed multiple: the sign choice makes the longitudinal cross term nonnegative, and the transverse inball displacement gives a uniform squared-distance surplus. It yields the required union increment even at sharp vertices. Small relative changes of radius are absorbed by their vanishing shell volume.

The finite-facet slack argument handles nonsimple vertices: any simultaneously near facets have a common face, with distance to that face controlled by their slack sum. Outside enlarged vertex balls, only a single facet or an edge pair remains. Their product-coordinate inclusions lose O(r) tangential measure, and the edge Jacobian is exactly the reciprocal sine of the interior angle. The half-space volume has derivative pi at zero, while the wedge has derivative pi/2 in each face-distance coordinate. The resulting Laplace integrals and powers of n/log n give constant intensity per facet area in the obtuse regime and per minimum-angle edge length in the acute regime. Every competing stratum has a strictly negative n exponent; vertex mass is bounded by a logarithm times an exponentially small factor.

The exact binomial factorial integral includes the necessary pairwise isolation compatibility. For separated centers the logarithm error is uniformly small. For close compatible clusters, choose the minimum clipped-volume center as root: an edge's pair volume is at least its smaller individual volume plus a fixed radius-cubed increment, and this smaller volume is at least the root volume. Root integration is bounded by the first-moment calculation; other centers cost only powers of radius-cubed. The extra exponential beats every fixed logarithmic factor. Thus all factorial moments tend to the Poisson moments. Finite Bonferroni sums prove the void limits without an unverified moment-determinacy shortcut.

The two category maxima then have independent continuous Gumbel-type limits. Comparing their strict and nonstrict values retains possible mutual-nearest-neighbor ties; the limiting diagonal has zero mass. Empty maxima and categories of zero/one limiting measure are dealt with separately. This establishes the distribution of the actual selected point, rather than just the radius or first-moment localization.
