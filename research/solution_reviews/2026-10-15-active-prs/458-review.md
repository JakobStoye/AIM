## Blocking finding: the corrected comparison theorem has extra hypotheses

The manuscript's **Section 3.3, source lines 677–725, PDF pp.9–10**, states and applies Theorem 8.2 of Kalton–Kunstmann–Weis (2006). Its version of the criterion assumes a complemented interpolation couple, sectoriality/calculus and two surjective projection-image identities on fractional scales. The application checks the positive image at lines 702–709 and the negative image using domain inclusion, duality and Hahn–Banach at lines 710–719. It then concludes channel Stokes calculus at lines 723–730.

There is a directly relevant published correction: **P. Kunstmann and L. Weis, *Erratum to: Perturbation and interpolation theorems for the H-infinity-calculus with applications to differential operators*, Math. Ann. 357 (2013), 801–804, DOI [10.1007/s00208-011-0768-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00208-011-0768-7)**; [author PDF](https://kaltonmemorial.missouri.edu/assets/docs/ma2006a.pdf). **Theorem 1.3, p.802**, replaces 2006 Theorem 8.2 and additionally requires compatible right inverses on both the base spaces and the two fractional endpoint scales. The correction explicitly distinguishes this from mere projection-image surjectivity.

No such compatible family of right inverses and fractional estimates is supplied in Section 3.3. Hahn–Banach gives the surjection used there; it does not provide that additional compatible family. In the erratum's Stokes application, the right inverse is the scalar Dirichlet Laplacian composed with inclusion and the inverse Stokes operator. **Lemma 2.2, p.803**, checks a small positive fractional endpoint by proving regularity above spatial order two. The manuscript establishes the channel domain at spatial order two and a subtrace image identity, but not this further right-inverse regularity. Its statement of the comparison criterion therefore omits a substantive hypothesis, and its present application has not verified the corrected theorem.

The physical domain is smooth, so the erratum's bounded-domain Theorem 2.1 can support the physical isotropic calculus after correcting that citation. It does not directly justify the separately treated periodic-channel geometry: the manuscript itself correctly observes at lines 657–658 that the bounded Euclidean-domain theorem cannot simply be invoked there. A direct channel calculus theorem, or an actual verification of the corrected right-inverse estimates on the channel, would close this particular gap. Neither is added silently by this review.

This is consequential. The unverified isotropic channel calculus is used at **lines 732–774** for weak-scale identifications and the additive stochastic channel estimate. The frozen-channel transport estimate then enters physical localization and the a priori estimate at **lines 1203–1215**, which supports continuation to arbitrary admissible amplitude at **lines 1249–1264**. The nonlinear fixed point consequently cannot establish its full claimed arbitrary-amplitude result from the submitted chain of arguments.

## Mathematical content checked independently

The following checks explain the useful progress and the scope of the remaining concern. They do not certify conclusions downstream of the missing channel-calculus input.

The weak spaces are the dual of zero-vector-trace solenoidal test fields and the positive no-slip Sobolev space, not a projected full-vector Sobolev space with an unproved tangential trace. Pruss–Wilke Section 5 and Proposition 5.1 identify the isotropic bounded-domain interpolation scale. The chosen Besov regularity lies above the boundary trace threshold. Theorem 1.1 has the requested finite smooth noise family, every bounded smooth three-dimensional domain, positive viscosity and only stochastic parabolicity, without an extra interior rank assumption.

In the channel calculation, the heat and pressure roots can collide at a positive resolvent parameter. I checked the confluent determinant and the divided-difference formulas, the divergence/pressure elimination, the full-line kernel and its distributional identity term. The weighted boundary inverse retains the small normal response needed when the heat frequency greatly exceeds the pressure frequency. The first and mixed second horizontal derivatives, Hardy bounds and Hilbert-index uniformity are the relevant ingredients for the claimed resolvent multiplier estimates. The full-torus model separately treats arbitrary-rank constant noise and the constant spatial mode, avoiding an inappropriate planar interior restriction.

The stochastic translation calculation includes the deterministic correction from translating the additive noise. Dropping it would change the equation, but it is retained. The matrix form of stochastic parabolicity produces the correct reduced diffusion. The general anisotropic additive construction is by deterministic correction of the isotropic stochastic solution; it does not assume an unproved general anisotropic square-root theorem. On the channel, however, the isotropic stochastic input still depends on the blocking calculus step.

The energy-form localizer is bounded on both positive zero-trace and weak dual spaces through its adjoint form. Its comparison with ordinary Piola transport retains the nonorthogonal pressure term. The negative-order pressure and zero-extension exponents are strictly within the primary source's windows at both dual exponents. The regularized supported divergence solver is applied on fixed convex Euclidean boxes rather than presuming a periodic Bogovskii theorem. The two fractional defect bounds are both included before the quantitative square-root perturbation argument. Conditional on the claimed isotropic channel scale, the triangular root comparison and the identity upgrading its bound to the positive scale are consistent. The geometric smallness survives the cover: the factor from the number of patches leaves a positive power of the patch radius. Compact remainders are absorbed using the physical energy norm rather than discarded.

The physical energy calculation is valid in the complete stochastic-parabolicity range. Helmholtz orthogonality in the energy space and boundary tangency make the homogeneous transport martingale vanish, while the quadratic variation leaves positive gradient coercivity. The continuation argument actually constructs a solution operator from a uniform bound, rather than asserting existence solely from an a priori inequality. Its problem is the unverified flat stochastic estimate feeding that uniform bound.

The nonlinear product estimate is subcritical in the stated Besov space, which embeds in the required Lebesgue space. The state cutoff is predictable and globally Lipschitz, and the local exit time is positive by continuity. **Section 6.2, lines 1340–1387**, independently proves uniqueness among any two existing adapted solutions in the entire requested path class: moment stopping legitimizes the variational identity; convection is handled by integration by parts and the available time-integrated boundedness; stochastic parabolicity gives a pathwise Gronwall inequality. This argument does not require the missing channel calculus or a probability-moment assumption on competing solutions. The common-stop construction in Section 6.3 is also logically correct conditional on the constructed linear solution operator: a smaller base threshold and a larger perturbed threshold imply that the probability of failing to reach the fixed base stop tends to zero. It does not assume positivity of an infinite infimum of exit times.

## Catalogue wording and required corrections

**Known cases:** The zero-noise deterministic case and sufficiently small transport noise follow from the bounded-domain isotropic additive estimate and a contraction in the displayed path space. Without any additional amplitude smallness, Section 6.2 proves pathwise uniqueness among existing solutions in that class. The manuscript also provides explicit flat-model and localization calculations, including an independent full-torus model; these model geometries alone are not solutions of the arbitrary bounded-domain target.

**Remaining target:** Establish local adapted existence and continuous dependence in the stated Besov/Sobolev class on every bounded smooth three-dimensional no-slip domain throughout the stochastic-parabolicity range. In this proposed proof, justify the periodic-channel isotropic calculus by a valid theorem or verify the compatible fractional right-inverse estimates required by the corrected comparison theorem, and then recheck the dependent linear estimates.
