The proof treats every strip width strictly below the spherical singularity, every real boundary parameter, and every integer mode. I checked the coordinate, potential and both outward-independent derivative signs against Siegl's original problem. The real Robin form is the square of the first-order expression plus the nonnegative mode term, so the initial spectral positivity used in the product argument holds also for the zero mode.

The central ingredient is strict interlacing of the full real Robin and Dirichlet spectra. Ordinary form-domain restrictions give one direction. For nonzero modes I reconstructed the rectangle-contour formula for the Fourier coefficients of the secant-squared potential, including the vertical-side signs and positive imaginary-axis kernel. Transferring cosine coefficients to sine coefficients preserves the derivative energy on the entire relevant Sobolev form domains. The resulting potential and endpoint difference is an integral of a nonzero power-series square against a strictly positive weight. Approximation and Cauchy--Schwarz justify this identity beyond finite sums. Min--max on finite-dimensional eigenspaces gives strict comparison. In the integer-frequency sector the mean-zero codimension condition gives the needed index shift. These two parity comparisons give the asserted full interlacing, rather than only paritywise inequalities.

The zero mode is handled separately and correctly. With the unrestricted first-order domain, its adjoint has zero endpoint traces; the two products are exactly the zero-mode Robin and mode-one Dirichlet operators. The first product has its one-dimensional kernel. Its positive eigenvalues coincide with those of the second product, and the difference of the two Dirichlet potentials is at least one. This supplies the missing strict zero-mode comparison without applying the positive secant-squared coefficient argument where its sign fails.

I checked the Volterra growth estimates and the derivative-with-respect-to-spectral-parameter identities proving simple zeros of the entire characteristic factors. Their order is at most one half. The shifted genus-zero products therefore have no extra nonconstant exponential factor. The deliberately unbalanced finite products have positive pole residues and a negative affine slope; they converge locally away from the poles. Their upper-half-plane imaginary parts are negative, and the nonconstant limiting holomorphic function has strictly negative imaginary part by the open mapping theorem. The complex Robin determinant equals twice the sum of the Robin product and the boundary-parameter square times the Dirichlet product. Hence it cannot vanish off the real axis. Finally the manuscript constructs the inhomogeneous Green solution and bounded map from $`L^2`$ to the exact $`H^2`$ domain for every nonreal spectral parameter. The graph-limit argument then proves closedness. Thus the conclusion concerns the full spectrum, not merely absence of nonreal eigenvalues.
