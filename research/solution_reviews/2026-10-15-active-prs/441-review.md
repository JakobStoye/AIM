The optical datum is nonzero and smooth with compact support; the density datum is zero. Both equations and optical transport are retained. Thus one finite-time singularity disproves the actual universal target; the manuscript does not claim failure for every parameter.

The scalar limit is taken before selecting linear constants, fixing one scalar profile and its similarity parameter. Rescaling each nearby coupled profile fixes that same parameter; the frequency change is kept explicitly. The resulting coefficient differences are small in precisely the multiplier norms used later. The scalar profile is nowhere zero by the radial imaginary-part identity. Its nonzero leading tail follows from the strict polynomial endpoint inequality. For the doubled real system, the conjugation symmetry and matrix signs agree with Li's operator.
