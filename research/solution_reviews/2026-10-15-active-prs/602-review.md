[Proof](../../solutions/602-surface-girth-facial-linear-bound/paper/manuscript.pdf).

The regular-neighborhood capping calculation cannot increase Euler genus. Contracting disjoint trees joined to a hub in the designated disk face preserves the hub cyclic label order. No resulting face walk has length two: two distinct spokes would repeat adjacent labels, and a doubled bridge would require hub degree one. Euler's formula therefore gives the stated alternation bound for both surface types.

For the trace lemma, the group site a is farthest within each equal-cardinality trace. A site b entering from a predecessor trace is strictly farther than a at every negative representative and no farther at each positive representative. If the two shortest-path trees met, their distance inequalities would contradict each other. Their selected representatives alternate, so the hub bound limits the occurrences of b. Summation gives the cubic trace count on sites anywhere in the graph, hence a bound for the correct primal ball-hypergraph VC dimension.

Boundary diameter makes equal-radius balls pairwise intersect. Minor density is bounded by the minimum of the simple-graph and Euler bounds. The fractional packing theorem then gives a fractional cover of order square-root genus. The VC covering inequality and the explicit logarithmic estimates give the claimed O(g) number of centers, with ample slack in the final constant.

I checked the noncellular reduction: if the whole facial boundary is a nonseparating cycle, its complement is the face and the graph is that cycle; otherwise capping the empty side gives a disk face without changing graph distances or increasing Euler genus. One subdivision doubles girth and leaves boundary diameter at most twice the chosen radius. Each induced ball is a tree, because any extra BFS edge would create a cycle shorter than the doubled girth. Nearest-center regions contain root geodesics, with consistent tie breaking; hence their spanning trees are disjoint. Every boundary run has at most twice the radius plus one vertices, and the hub bound controls the run count. The final division by two for subdivision gives a constant strictly below 100000 for every g and girth in the target.
