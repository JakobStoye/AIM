The scope is essential. The AIM statement requires a jointly smooth nonnegative solution with initially Schwartz derivatives and uniformly bounded local mass, energy and entropy. It does not impose energy conservation, an energy inequality, or positive-time Schwartz bounds as part of admissibility. The constructed homogeneous radial solution meets those explicit premises and develops a positive inverse-sixth-power velocity tail. It disproves the requested continuation *with rapid velocity decay*. It already continues smoothly in its polynomially weighted class and does not prove a derivative singularity or settle an energy-admissible continuation formulation.

I reconstructed the radial collision reduction with the specified full-sphere normalization and post-collision velocities. The gain/loss coefficients, the minimum-square-root energy kernel and its Jacobian give the printed constants. Separating the leading radial tail operator, cutting it off and subtracting its Gaussian mass gives a mass-preserving linear operator. Direct moment splitting gives the faster quadratic remainder from weight six to weight seven. Ordinary mass integrals are absolutely convergent and permit the collision interchange; the proof never incorrectly uses the divergent energy-weighted collision integral to assert energy conservation.

On the vanishing weighted-tail space with exponent strictly between six and seven, the compactly supported linear core has the claimed positive evolution, with gain/loss ratio at most seventeen eighteenths. Density, uniform weighted control and the bounded Gaussian rank-one perturbation supply the strongly continuous mass-preserving evolution. The explicit flat entrance solves the leading tail equation, is pointwise initially zero with all time jets zero, and has nonzero inverse-sixth-power tail at positive times. Its compactified mass correction has a defect in the faster weighted space. The defect bound uses the kernel equation, avoiding an invalid bound by the logarithmic derivative of the flat coefficient. The nonlinear variation-of-constants fixed point and differentiated integral equation yield a smooth faster-decaying correction; the generator-domain argument then gives the actual pointwise collision equation.

I checked the bootstrap at the radial origin in the energy variable and on every compact velocity interval. The integral coefficients and tame derivative bounds provide joint smoothness, including all initial jets, without silently assuming uniform Schwartz seminorms at positive times. Nonnegativity is not inferred from the mass-corrected linear evolution. Instead, the positive explicit tail dominates the faster remainder beyond a radius proportional to the inverse cube of time, making the negative part compactly supported at every positive time. The mixed Gaussian gain estimate has coefficient strictly below the available loss frequency. The upper Dini derivative of the weighted negative-part maximum is bounded by an integrable function of time times that maximum. Its initial flat bound tends to zero, so Gronwall gives nonnegativity. This checks the delicate global-velocity step of the counterexample.

Uniform weight-six control gives finite energy in three dimensions and a uniform pointwise bound; fixed mass one yields the positive lower mass bound and the entropy upper bound. The negative entropy part is integrable by comparison with a Gaussian and the finite second moment. For each fixed positive endpoint the tail coefficient is positive. Any smooth endpoint extension is continuous at each finite velocity and therefore inherits that same tail, excluding the requested rapid decay. All constants are fixed on a short interval and the homogeneous solution supplies the torus example.
